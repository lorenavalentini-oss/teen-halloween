01 · READING

Why do people celebrate Halloween?

Halloween is on 31 October. Today, many teenagers enjoy costumes, scary films and parties. But Halloween has a very old story.

More than 2,000 years ago, Celtic people celebrated a festival called Samhain (pronounced “SAH-win”). It marked the end of the harvest season and the beginning of winter. People believed that spirits could visit the world of the living. They lit fires and wore costumes.

Today, Halloween is popular in countries such as the United States, Canada and Ireland. Children often go trick-or-treating: they visit houses, say “Trick or treat!” and receive sweets. Some families decorate their homes with pumpkins, skeletons and spider webs.

What about teenagers? Many teens watch horror movies, make creative costumes, listen to spooky music or spend time with friends. Some people love scary stories, but others prefer funny costumes. Halloween is not the same for everyone!

Think & talk: Do you like scary movies? Is Halloween popular where you live? What costume do you want to wear?

03 · GRAMMAR

Quick grammar reminder

TO BE → I am a vampire. / She isn't scared. / Are they at the party?
PRESENT SIMPLE → I watch scary films. / He watches films. / She doesn't like spiders. / Do you celebrate Halloween?
Remember: he / she / it → verb + -s / -es in affirmative sentences; after does / doesn't, use the base verb.
